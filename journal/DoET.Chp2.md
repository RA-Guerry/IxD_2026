# Design of Everyday Things - Chapter 2 Response

## Execution and Evaluation
In **this chapter**, we distinguish the process humans go through to solve the problems in our life. This involves switching between *execution* and *evaluation*. When a problem comes up, we think of a solution, then execute that solution. Once we receive the result, we evaluate said result. If we evaluate we didn't get the result we want, we think of another solution, and the process repeats. This operates on both a conscious level when we're learning new things or solving difficult problems, and a subconscious level when we already understand the actions we're doing and the results we receive. The problem is when we run into something which does not function as we initially evaluated it would. Sometimes we have alternative solutions come to mind immediately, other times we're forced to evaluate for a long time, ask for help, or just give up trying to do the thing we wanted in the first place. This is a design failure of the device, not the person. 

## The Gulf
The goal of a design should be to make it easy to evaluate what to do, and work as expected when a person executes the task. **Page 38** has Don Norman, the book's author, mention "When people use something, they face two gulfs: the Gulf of Execution, where they try to figure out how it operates, and the Gulf
of Evaluation, where they try to figure out what happened. The role of the designer is to help people bridge the two gulfs." All designs should try to make these gulfs small, regardless of skill level. A design which is meant to be complicated or challenging to some degree should ideally be designed only slightly above the average skill level of a person using the device. This doesn't mean that, for example, the controls of an entire power plant should be operational to every person who could enter the plant, but someone who's hired for the job shouldn't need to sit confused and take several weeks to learn how such an important control panel works. Things which are used every day should be especially easy to figure out. When a person struggles with something as simple as a TV remote, even a remote like this:
